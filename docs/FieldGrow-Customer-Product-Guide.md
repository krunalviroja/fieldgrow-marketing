## Executive summary

FieldGrow connects the work of a service business with the next opportunity to serve its customers. The intended operating model brings customer records, quotations, bookings, technician work, invoices, collections and repeat-service reminders together. Owners can understand what needs attention without relying on separate conversations, spreadsheets and paper records.

For an Indian business with 5–20 technicians, the practical goal is simple: organize today's service work, protect the payment trail and make it easier for customers to return. The Service-to-Growth Engine™ connects completed work with feedback, reputation, maintenance reminders and repeat bookings.

The current reviewed project is a marketing website with interactive sample demonstrations. It does not establish that a production business account, operational database, payment gateway or technician application is available. This guide explains both the confirmed demonstrations and the requested operational model. Proposed workflows are clearly identified; they are not instructions for screens that already exist.

All people, amounts, performance figures and service scenarios in this guide are illustrative unless explicitly identified as a calculation. They are not verified customer outcomes. Pricing for the FieldGrow subscription is not established by the reviewed material.

## Part 1 — Introduction

### What is FieldGrow?

FieldGrow is positioned as a Field Service Management and Business Growth Platform. It is designed around a service business's customer lifecycle: understand the request, prepare a quotation, confirm the visit, assign the right technician, document the work, invoice correctly, record payment and offer the next relevant service.

Its business operating system concept means keeping connected records rather than replacing every tool with an isolated new tool. A booking should refer to a customer; the job should refer to the booking; materials and costs should refer to the job; an invoice should explain the work; the next reminder should use the customer's actual service history.

### Vision, mission and intended customers

Vision: a service business that can run reliably without the owner personally coordinating every action. Mission: give small and growing Indian service teams a practical way to organize work, understand job economics and earn repeat business through timely service.

The primary audience is owners with 5–20 technicians. Admin staff, dispatchers, technicians, accountants and customers participate in the same service lifecycle. Owners can begin a walkthrough with one service category, one team and one complete booking-to-payment example.

### Industries and expected advantages

The positioning covers AC/HVAC/appliances, CCTV/security/networking, pest control, cleaning, electrical, plumbing, home services, facilities maintenance and other businesses that send people to customer locations. Industry-specific configuration and production support must be confirmed individually.

Expected advantages are clearer job ownership, shared customer history, fewer forgotten appointments, documented collection, better visibility of direct job costs and more consistent maintenance follow-up. Results depend on adoption, accurate data and the confirmed operational release—not simply on visiting the website.

Customer → Quotation → Booking → Job assignment → Technician service → Invoice → Payment → Feedback → Repeat booking

## Part 3 — Product modules

### 3.1 Admin web and mobile command center

Overview and purpose: give the owner and office staff a daily picture of jobs, people and money. The problem is fragmented coordination: a schedule in one place, payment information in another and technician updates inside private chats.

Status: Demonstration Only for the illustrated control-room dashboard and before/after cleanup interaction in current code. A live operations view should make upcoming, active, delayed and completed work distinguishable; production states and mobile access require confirmation.

Suggested workflow: review today's bookings; identify unassigned or delayed jobs; allocate work; monitor updates; resolve exceptions; review invoicing and collections; assign tomorrow's actions. This is an intended operating procedure, not an existing screen-by-screen tutorial.

Example: an eight-technician AC business starts the day by checking booked visits, an absent employee and pending collections. The dispatcher adjusts assignments while the owner reviews maintenance opportunities. Benefit: fewer status calls and clearer responsibility. Roles: owner, admin, branch manager, dispatcher and accountant.

## Part 4 — Service-to-Growth Engine™

FieldGrow's central concept is that completed work contains the information needed for the next useful customer conversation. The opportunity might be a review, a maintenance reminder, a contract renewal or a relevant service offer—not an unrelated promotional message.

Customer → Quotation → Booking → Assignment → Service → Invoice → Payment → Review → Marketing → Repeat booking

### How the loop works

1. Record the service outcome and customer/asset history so the business knows what happened.
2. Ask for feedback and provide a review link after service where the communication integration is available. Invite honest reviews rather than selecting only satisfied customers for a public request.
3. Determine the next appropriate service based on the actual service interval and contract terms. Review suggestions before making a promise.
4. Contact existing customers about due maintenance or renewal through an approved channel.
5. With permission, reuse suitable work photos and service descriptions as social content. Check the message before publishing.
6. Confirm any repeat visit as a new booking with scope and availability, then measure the resulting paid work separately from estimated opportunity.

Review delivery, recurring schedules, intelligent recommendations, referral campaigns and automatic reactivation are Planned / Integration Required. The website illustrates parts of this loop; it does not execute it end to end against real customer records.

### Practical example: one AC visit, another future service

Ramesh Patel requests AC maintenance in Ahmedabad. The business checks the customer record, agrees a ₹999 service quotation and books the visit. Rahul completes the checklist, records service evidence and explains any additional work before charging for it. The office issues the invoice and confirms payment.

After the visit, the intended workflow asks for feedback and shares a Google review link. If appropriate, the business notes a six-month service interval. Before that date, it reviews a maintenance reminder and an illustrative ₹1,999 annual package offer. The customer accepts only if the scope and price suit them; a new booking starts the next cycle.

Business benefit: a service record supports a timely, relevant conversation. A reminder is not a booking, and a booking is not collected revenue. The amounts and timing above are examples, not published FieldGrow subscription prices or promised sales.

### Revenue Recovery Simulator

Status: Available as a working website calculation; its outputs are Demonstration Only estimates. Configurable inputs are technician count (3–40), monthly jobs (80–1,200 in steps of 10), and average job value (₹800–₹8,000 in steps of ₹100).

The current calculation is: dormant-customer estimate = monthly jobs × average job value × 12%; quotation estimate = monthly jobs × average job value × 8%; AMC estimate = technicians × 1.5 × average job value × 10%. The displayed total is the rounded sum.

At the default inputs of 8 technicians, 320 jobs and ₹2,500 average value: reactivation ₹96,000 + quotations ₹64,000 + AMC ₹3,000 = ₹1,63,000 estimated monthly recovery. These assumptions are fixed in the code; sliders do not replace a financial assessment of the business. The sample ₹1,55,500 opportunity list is separate from this calculator.

Workflow: choose approximate inputs; compare scenarios; discuss which customers and estimates actually qualify; apply realistic response and completion expectations; assess direct costs before treating any amount as profit. No result is guaranteed and no campaign is sent from the calculator.

### 10.1 Customer enquiry to customer record

3. Confirm the correct person, service address, asset and permission to communicate.

### 10.2 Customer request to quotation

Request → Clarify scope → Estimate service/materials → Review charges → Share quotation

1. Check history and ask for the information needed to define work.
2. Arrange inspection when a remote estimate would be unreliable.
3. List labour, materials, exclusions, applicable taxes and discounts where supported.
4. Review the version and share it through a supported channel.

Example/output: a camera replacement quotation separates the device from installation. Owner: quotation executive with technician input. Status: Planned; actual document sharing may require integration.

### 10.3 Quotation approval to booking

Quotation → Customer decision → Approved scope → Available appointment → Confirmed booking

1. Confirm the customer accepted the current version; revise rather than overwrite an agreed scope.

3. Agree the service address and appointment window.

Example/output: approved AC repair is booked for a realistic window. Owner: admin/customer and dispatcher. Status: Planned.

Booking → Work order → Checklist/material needs → Assignment-ready job

4. Check the job is ready before assigning it.

Example/output: cleaning scope becomes a room checklist. Owner: admin/dispatcher. Status: Planned.

### 10.5 Job assignment to technician

Ready job → Check skills/availability → Assign → Confirm receipt → Customer update

1. Compare service skill and expected duration with team capacity.
2. Consider travel and access constraints.
3. Assign and confirm the technician has seen the instructions.
4. Communicate a realistic arrival window; revise it if work changes.

Example/output: a qualified technician accepts a CCTV visit. Owner: dispatcher and technician. Status: Planned; navigation/tracking and delivery require integration.

Completion record → Review scope/costs → Approve charges → Issue invoice → Share

2. Review actual materials against quoted inclusions.
3. Apply verified billing/tax configuration.

Example/output: the invoice explains service and approved replacement part. Owner: admin/accountant. Status: Planned; GST and sharing configuration require confirmation.

Invoice → Receive payment → Record method/collector → Allocate amount → Receipt → Reconcile

2. Accept a supported payment method or record a collection.

4. Confirm actual receipt/settlement rather than trusting a customer screenshot alone.

Example/output: a genuine review or a documented issue to resolve. Owner: admin/customer. Status: Integration Required and Planned; no actual review service verified.
